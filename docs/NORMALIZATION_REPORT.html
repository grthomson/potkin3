CK-certified contraction postponement

12 October 2026 · POTKIN3 research continuation · Paper theorems and executable finite checks

Decision: develop a proof-producing, cost-aware version of the Section 4 contraction-postponement construction, carrying exact CK observations and contextual replacement certificates. This is a concrete D4/D3 advance with D5 payoff. It preserves Cut-freeness under stated assumptions; it does not yet eliminate logical Cut.

1. What has advanced

The canonical uniform-depth expansion in Ciabattoni–Genco, Lemmas 4.6–4.7, can stop a branch as soon as a supplied premise proof fits its boundary. For multiplicities (1,M), this reduces the expansion from 2M supplied-proof copies to M+1. Counting weakening reveals that stopping immediately is not always best. A finite stop/continue recurrence chooses a smallest proof within a precisely stated plan language. CK cut spectra then distinguish equally small normalization plans, and the distinction composes through surrounding contexts.

These are developments of the paper’s explicit construction. We do not claim that early stopping or dynamic programming is a new idea in the literature. The research contribution proposed here is their typed integration with the occurrence-sensitive CK/context framework and its verified replacement infrastructure.

2. Relation to Section 4

In Section 4.1 the condition on two top-rule applications means: two applications belonging to the same 2-system instance must not occur on one path. Thus the normalized condition excludes two or more. These are top-rule occurrences, not logical Cut rules and not already CK cuts. “2-system” means two levels of rules, not two cuts. An antichain interpretation of those selected occurrences is a useful bridge, but requires a map from rule occurrences to the actual CK cut positions and preservation of instance/scope labels.

Section 4.2.1 offers an immediately executable mathematical target: move external contractions to a terminal queue using a context-sharing rule and external weakening. Our work below strengthens the choice of expansion in that construction. It does not yet include the full structured-form theorem, its subsequent weakening permutations, or the complete translation to a 2-system.

Source: Ciabattoni–Genco, Hypersequents and Systems of Rules: Embeddings and Applications, especially Lemmas 4.6–4.8, read from the supplied attachment. Existing analytic embeddings remain the proof-theoretic orientation. Lang’s revival and the focusing comparison remain separate adapters, not silently identified calculi.

3. Typed construction and proof

Fix n≥1, positive integers mᵢ, and a rule schema r with premises G|Cᵢ (one active component in each premise) and conclusion G|H. The schema must admit the same instance under every external-context extension used below. Merely calling a rule “context-sharing” does not discharge side conditions. Assume one-component external weakening EW and external contraction EC. Supplied derivations Dᵢ conclude G|G′ᵢ|Cᵢmᵢ; each component of G′ᵢ occurs in G. Let G″ be the multiset union of the G′ᵢ, and q=1+Σᵢ(mᵢ−1).

For α∈ℕⁿ with |α|≤q, define
Bα = G | G″ | H^(q−|α|) | ∏ᵢ Cᵢ^αᵢ.

STOP at α using i, whenever αᵢ≥mᵢ:
  obtain Bα by EW from Dᵢ.

CONTINUE at α, whenever |α|<q:
  recursively derive every B(α+eᵢ), then apply r
  with common context G | G″ | H^(q−|α|−1) | ∏ᵢ Cᵢ^αᵢ.

Existence and typing theorem. Every plan obeying these choices derives B₀. Proof is induction on q−|α|. The stop case uses a specified multiset inclusion of the donor boundary. In the continue case the i-th child is exactly the common context plus Cᵢ, so the rule yields Bα. At depth q some αᵢ≥mᵢ by pigeonhole, so an allowed stop always exists. Finally contract the designated extra copies in G″ and Hq to obtain G|H.

If all Dᵢ are EC-free, the only EC nodes introduced are in the final queue. If all donors are logical-Cut-free and r is not logical Cut, the resulting derivation is logical-Cut-free. This is preservation, not a general cut-elimination theorem. Copying donors is literal copying into distinct tree occurrences.

4. First-hit improvement and its limits

Always stop at the first threshold. The internal profiles are exactly 0≤αᵢ<mᵢ. A profile α occurs at multinomial(|α|;α) distinct physical addresses. Therefore

I = Σ(0≤αᵢ<mᵢ) |α|! / ∏ᵢ αᵢ!       [number of r nodes]
L = 1 + (n−1)I                           [number of donor copies].
For n=2: I = binomial(m₁+m₂,m₁)−1.
For (m₁,m₂)=(1,M): I=M and L=M+1.
Uniform depth M instead gives I=2^M−1 and L=2^M.

These count the expansion skeleton, not all proof nodes. Donor copies and EW/EC nodes must be included separately. Under fresh, pairwise distinct active component labels absent from G,G′ᵢ,H, first-hit minimizes the number of r applications in the opaque-donor r/EW language: along any competing branch ending at Dᵢ, at least mᵢ choices of premise i are required; backwards weakening only deletes components. Pruning every competing branch at its first threshold leaves the first-hit tree. This argument fails if components alias or additional rules are allowed.

Let gᵢ=|G″|−|G′ᵢ|, h=|H|, dᵢ=number of positive nodes in Dᵢ. A canonical stop adds exactly wᵢ(α)=gᵢ+(q−|α|)h+|α|−mᵢ one-component EW nodes. This is the count for the specified inclusion/script; aliases may permit better scripts outside this language. The common final EC queue has c=|G″|+(q−1)h nodes.

Obstruction: for (1,2), empty G′ᵢ, first-hit size minus uniform-depth size is h−3−d₁. Large H can make first-hit larger. With (2,2) and supplied one-node donor shapes, h=1 gives 17 versus 23 nodes; h=20 gives 55 versus 23. Thus first-hit alone is not an adequate optimization policy.

5. Exact optimal plan recurrence

V(α) = min of:
  dᵢ + wᵢ(α)                    for each eligible donor i;
  1 + Σᵢ V(α+eᵢ)               if |α|<q.
Total output size = V(0)+c.

Optimality theorem. V(α) is the least number of nodes among the canonical depth-at-most-q stop/continue plans at α. The minimum is attained by a typed plan. Proof: well-founded induction. Every plan is either one of the listed stops or a rule node with independent child plans. Additivity bounds each continuation by the sum of the child minima, and selecting witnesses attains that bound. Both first-hit and uniform expansion are included, so the optimum is no larger than either.

This is not global proof minimization or a claim about arbitrary logical derivations. There are at most binomial(q+n,n) profiles; arithmetic and polynomial costs still depend on integer sizes and spectrum degrees. A memoized computation is an evaluator/compiler implementation, not a DAG proof carrier. Its output unfolds as a tree, potentially very large.

6. Where the CK framework does useful work

For the full structural CK cut family of a positive rooted tree T, define QT(z)=Σcz|Pᶜ(T)|, including the empty cut and the full/root cut. Selected factors are counted with physical multiplicity. A typed Box has Q=1 but remains a typed state, not the coefficient unit. At a positive node, Q=z+∏Qchild. Consequently:

Stop:      Qα(z) = QDi(z) + wᵢ(α)z.
Continue:  Qα(z) = z + ∏ᵢ Q(α+eᵢ)(z).
Final EC:  Qout(z) = Q0(z) + cz.

This is a scalar observation of CK extraction; it is not the entire coproduct. It retains exact cut multiplicities and widths, but forgets factor identities and remainders. Memoizing it never identifies physical cuts. For scope-filtered eligibility, the recurrence needs an additional validity theorem/interface state; do not apply the full-structural recurrence blindly to the native scoped registry.

One can minimize the lexicographic pair (proof nodes, Q(1)). Induction works because child node counts add and cut counts are positive and multiply, followed by adding one. An alternative is a Pareto frontier of size and coefficientwise spectra. Neither objective is a theorem that fewer cuts universally means better semantics. The practical interpretation is smaller proof certificates and fewer full CK decompositions to enumerate in an extraction-based analysis.

7. Equal-size proofs, strictly different CK cost

Take n=2, m=(1,2), q=2, empty shared context, and a supplied context-stable rule with four conclusion components. Take D₁ to be an identity proof of C₁ and D₂ an identity proof followed by EW deriving C₂|C₂. Thus Q(D₁)=1+z and Q(D₂)=1+2z. 7a. A concrete communication-rule instance

The size improvement also has a direct instance in the paper’s HLJ+(com), rather than only an assumed four-component rule. Set C₁=(A⇒A), C₂=(B⇒B), and H=(B⇒A)|(A⇒B), with empty external and internal contexts. This is the communication rule with Φ=A, Ψ=B, Π₁=A, Π₂=B. Set m=(2,2). Each donor is identity followed by EW, deriving two copies of its identity sequent, hence is EC-free and Cut-free. Then q=3 and h=2.

The opaque-donor restriction is essential to the optimization theorem.

8. Contextual reuse and signed defects

Context theorem. For each fixed one-hole positive proof context C, Q(C[P])=aC+bCQ(P) with nonnegative polynomial coefficients. At the hole, a=0,b=1. At a node on the hole path, multiply the child expression by the spectra of all fixed siblings and add z. This proves the assertion by induction; composition gives bC∘D=bCbD.

Hence Q(C[P])−Q(C[P′])=bC(Q(P)−Q(P′)). Coefficientwise dominance, and total-cut dominance at z=1, survive every fixed compatible exterior. Proof size gains also survive a one-hole replacement. Multiple distinct holes give a nonnegative multiaffine polynomial, so simultaneous dominance composes when the fillings are jointly admissible. Correlated scope certificates are not licensed by mere forest multiplication.

At the full packet level, the already reported native law δGC=KCδ gives the signed replacement equation δGC(P−P′)=KCδ(P−P′), once these hypersequent proofs and contexts have a valid native adapter. This is a proposed application of the verified law, not a newly executed Lean theorem here.

Positivity must not be assumed for arbitrary normalization. The audit found an EC/EW relocation with Qnew−Qold=z(1+X₁−X₂), where Xᵢ are donor spectra. It can decrease or increase total cuts. The signed K action is therefore the right general transport device. Normalization need not preserve the coproduct, and the antipode is not a normalizer.

10. Evidence and next gate

Three independent research agents covered the typed construction, CK consequences, and adversarial review. Their notes and executable checks accompany this report. The scripts check 39 threshold shapes, exact multinomial counts, small physical antichain enumeration, full EW/source-node reversal examples, and 56 finite stop/continue optimization instances against exhaustive attainable score sets. The latter also checks component-multiset boundaries. Equal-size spectra were independently recalculated. These checks support debugging; universal claims above rest on the stated paper arguments.

No new Lean verification was run. The user’s reported native gluing and K-action results are treated as verified input, not re-audited checkout contents. The next gate is one additive native Lean compiler module proving boundary correctness, EC placement, Cut-free preservation, and the size recurrence; then one observation module proving the CK recurrence and context dominance. First instantiate a real rule admitted by the project. Do not begin by packaging a generic Hopf algebra or rerunning previously completed gluing.

Open limitations: arbitrary noncrossing cut schedules, whole-calculus normalization, full 2-system translation, native scope-aware spectra, and antipode-based applications remain unproved. The four-component tie fixture is schematic. The concrete communication example above is paper-level; a native registered-rule optimization fixture is a required next deliverable, not a completed result.
