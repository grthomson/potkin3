# What a signature certificate guarantees for module verification

This note isolates the consequence needed by a verification client. It assumes the mixed finite-mode theorem proved in `mixed_theory.md`; finite executable evidence is recorded separately. All material coefficients retain physical occurrence identities, complete boundary types and persistent seams. Detached forests use the declared commutative observation.

## Certified operations and clients

Let `[t]` be the whole-signature class of an open proof operation. A client state comprises this class together with its complete interface. Permitted client actions are:

1. Apply a jointly typed linear implementation to free inputs.
2. Select an actual material antichain, retaining its full forest coefficient and the corresponding complete residual operation.
3. Fill named actual material-origin holes with jointly typed implementations.
4. Place the result in a declared typed proof context.

Extraction may also continue inside a detached factor. Those factors agree literally, with marks, so their internal extractions agree directly. This is essential for a two-stage flag extracting a composite module and then opening its internal components.

An extraction request is indexed by a rigid actual forest, rather than just the number or names of primitive leaves. In this occurrence-linear setting its coefficient identifies the corresponding residual. A request using an unmarked shape instead must retain all multiplicities and outcomes; there is no permission to choose one arbitrary matching occurrence.

## Finite-history transfer theorem

If `Sigma(t)=Sigma(u)`, then every finite well-typed client history from `t` has a matching history from `u`, with equal full extraction coefficients, equal residual signature classes, and equal ordinary logical observations. The converse matching direction holds as well.

Proof: input implementation, context insertion and hole filling preserve signature equality by the finite-mode theorem. The comodule property gives matching forest coefficients and residual classes for extraction. Apply these facts inductively to the length of the client history. For nested extractions, the actual coaction equation identifies the two parenthesizations, including the detached intermediate material factors; this is the CK flag law. Literal equality of detached factors handles actions inside them. The ordinary observation is a readout of the class. No finite upper bound on future history length is used. Endpoints remain nonzero complete operations. QED.

This also permits adaptive histories: whenever the next action is chosen as a function of already matched observations and interfaces, the same action is available on both sides. A choice inspecting raw syntax beyond the certified observations is outside the theorem.

## Verification payoff

A module assembly requirement can ask whether a specified *intact composite* is extractable and whether its remainder satisfies a predicate on the normalized complete operation. The theorem transfers such a requirement after any permitted history. More generally it transfers every predicate on the matched observation history, including the non-reachability of an explicitly designated bad **assembly observation**.

This is a reachability statement about the declared assembly transition system. It is not, without a further adequate operational interpretation, a process-runtime deadlock or memory-safety theorem. The verifier should expose the exact composite requested, complete remainder, and nested flag that establish its result.

There are two distinct certificate levels. A finite signature comparison certifies all admitted future implementations by the paper theorem. A portable certificate of a particular implementation and extraction flag validates that concrete instance by recomputing its typing and actual cuts. Checking the latter does not mechanize the universal theorem. Keeping these levels explicit prevents finite examples from being presented as universal verification.

## Why leaf inventories are inadequate

The operations `r(s(a,b),c)` and `r(a,s(b,c))` can have the same marked leaf inventory and all primitive-leaf histories after a coarse support readout. Only the first supplies the intact factor `s(a,b)` and its internal flag with intermediate `s(hole_a,hole_b)`. The corresponding full material coefficient is nonzero on the first side and absent on the other. Consequently the assembly requirement above cannot be verified from those primitive tests alone.

When a symbolic probe is implemented by a composite proof, the new internal cuts must be expanded using the exact outer/inner Faà di Bruno law. Mapping a primitive probe directly to the composite coefficient as if this were a Hopf morphism would omit these cuts and invalidate the client guarantee. This is the substantive CK dependency of the result. An alternative presentation carrying all the same typed antichains and coherence data would be mathematically equivalent.

For fixed or nonmonotone material bases, formal failing modes are diagnostic until their joint complete-judgment witnesses are supplied. Accepted formal-mode equality remains a sufficient certificate. This note neither assumes conservative nullary-probe extensions nor promotes old nonmaterial rules to material.
