# Sufficient native eligibility adapter

Paper-level sufficient conditions, 12 October 2026. These conditions have not
been checked against POTKIN3's actual material-root predicate. They do not
assume the desired cut equivalence as a hypothesis.

## Local marked-vertex criterion

Use typed occurrence trees with literal rule labels, ordered premise slots,
and explicit inherited interface states. Suppose:

1. Each positive physical vertex has an eligibility mark determined by its
   rule label and inherited interface decoration. Formal holes are not vertices.
2. Installing a payload at a matching typed hole preserves these decorations,
   hence marks, on the payload, the context spine, and all sibling regions.
   The payload is understood at the hole's actual inherited state, not at a
   newly reset root state.
3. Replacing an extracted subtree by its same-state hole preserves decorations
   and marks on every surviving vertex. Detaching a subtree preserves its
   inherited external scope and all its vertex marks.
4. Native extraction cuts are exactly finite antichains of eligible physical
   vertices, with literal same-state holes and occurrence-preserving refill.

Condition 4 is a concrete carrier characterization, independently checkable
against the existing native cut/address theorem. It is not the context-cut
bijection that we seek to derive. Conditions 1–3 can alternatively be proved
as preservation lemmas for a non-Boolean native eligibility predicate.

## Consequence for one-hole contexts

Cuts of C[P] split uniquely into two cases: no selected spine vertex, or one
selected eligible spine vertex k. Consequently

    Cuts(C[P]) ≃
      Cuts(P) × product(all spine sibling cut sets)
      ⊔ sum(eligible spine k) product(sibling cut sets strictly above k).

Only the spine-index set changes from the all-positive theorem. Marks in every
factor are the inherited marks. In the second case all deeper vertices are
swallowed and supply no additional choice. Ordered pruning packets, literal
remainders and factor-to-hole matching follow from address-prefix bookkeeping.

The same argument proves the affine L/A laws and the counit-corrected K action.
Every nonempty pruning packet has positive degree, so the counit still kills
it; the empty cut remains unique. No requirement that every positive vertex
be eligible is needed. Typed context composition is restricted to substitutions
which preserve the stated interface decorations.

Condition 3 is additionally essential for cut flags. One compatible coefficient
Hopf carrier is generated by eligible-root trees, allowing ineligible internal
vertices, with extraction only at marked vertices. Every extracted factor has
an eligible root. Persistent marks make its coproduct close under remainders
and make the usual nested-cut bijection work. Grading by positive vertices is
connected after the coefficient identity-edge quotient. Arbitrary-root typed
trees can then be remainder states for a coaction over this carrier.

Do not instead combine restricted state cuts with an unrelated coefficient
coproduct that extracts every unmarked internal vertex: that generally fails
coassociativity. Either prove the coefficient/state eligibility comparison or
reuse an already established native coaction.

## Two minimal obstruction patterns

**Loss of inherited state under installation.** Let a vertex be eligible iff
its ambient depth is even. A standalone leaf at depth zero is eligible; the
same leaf installed under a unary context is at depth one and is ineligible.
Using the standalone cut set in the Cartesian product gives a false cut.
Retaining inherited depth parity in the hole and payload state repairs this
particular example. A local-state predicate is sufficient only when the state
is actually transported correctly.

**Loss of marks under residual formation.** Let a vertex be eligible iff it
has a positive-node child. In the unary chain r(a(b(c))), both a and b are
eligible. Selecting a and then selecting b inside that extracted factor is a
valid nested decomposition. Selecting b first leaves r(a(Box)); now a is
ineligible, so the opposite-order cut flag disappears. This defeats the usual
coassociativity bijection. Merely preserving eligibility under installation
would not repair residual instability.

Likewise, a sibling's eligibility that depends on the installed payload size
invalidates a fixed product of sibling cut sets unless that dependency is
explicitly included in a substitution-stable interface. The criterion excludes
such hidden dependence rather than assuming it away in the conclusion.

## Exact mechanization obligation

Locate the actual native eligibility definition and prove or refute the three
preservation properties on concrete rule instances. If one fails, identify
which additional inherited decoration restores it, or weaken the context class
explicitly. An abstract theorem under this criterion is useful infrastructure;
it is not yet a verified theorem about the native material predicate.
