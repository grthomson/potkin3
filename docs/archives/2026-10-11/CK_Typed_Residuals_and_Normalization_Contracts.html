Typed CK residuals with normalization-aware proof contracts

Main outcome. D4 now has an exact finite residual construction for a genuine typed CK coaction, together with a concrete logical application: universal contracts bounding the number of designated material operations in a proof’s normal form, even when exponential Cut duplicates or erases providers. The most fruitful companion is now a bounded D2 investigation. D1 remains a useful two-calculus comparison, and D5 supplies the interpretation as permitted correlated families of replacements.

This is a finite-registry theorem about unbounded derivation trees, not merely bounded tree enumeration. It does not establish general Cut elimination, derive an analytic sequent calculus, or verify a process calculus. The arithmetic and automata methods are established; the contribution candidate is their precise connection to typed CK decomposition and complete frontier contracts.

1. What changed from the previous round

The ordinary CK residual allowed arbitrary grafting and detached components around a remainder. Real proof rules have fixed premise ports. Simply adding type labels to the earlier recurrence would therefore have been wrong.

We separated three objects: the connected coefficient Hopf algebra, the typed carrier of proof remainders, and the occurrence-level cut witnesses needed for exact refilling. This led to a genuine typed coaction and an inverse-cut theorem. A separate finite recognizer then computes its universal residuals. The designated-hole recognizer was retained as a different, useful operation rather than silently identified with that residual.

The natural application also changed. A subexponential promotion-side-condition observer mostly repeats what exact typing already checks. A property of the normalized proof gives a stronger test: it depends on how providers and consumers interact, and cannot be recovered from the source’s material counts.

2. The carrier: CK coefficients, exact typed remainders

Fix exact judgment sorts and ordered rule instances, each with specified premise sorts and conclusion sort. Let B be the planar typed operadic-tree bialgebra. A zero-vertex edge eJ represents a vacant proof interface of judgment J. Its coproduct is eJ ⊗ eJ. Positive rule trees retain every input port.

Let q:B→H identify standalone zero-vertex edge factors with the forest unit. The quotient H is a connected CK-type Hopf algebra; positive trees retain their typed internal ports. Define

λ = (q ⊗ id) ΔB : B → H ⊗ B.

This is a counital coaction. The coefficient side is connected; the remainder side still distinguishes the exact interfaces. For a material ground g:()→J,

λ(g) = 1 ⊗ g + g ⊗ eJ.

Selecting a vertex-local class of material rules gives a Hopf projection π onto the pure-material coefficient algebra and a material coaction ρ=(π⊗id)λ. A root outside the selected class suppresses the whole-root material term. This retains the familiar proper CK antichains while treating the whole endpoint honestly.

The construction follows the polynomial-tree CK framework of Kock, with an explicit ordered flag argument. The quotient-left coaction is an elementary deduction. It is not an invented replacement for CK, nor a claim that the current native Lean carrier has already instantiated this linear theorem.

Two corrections. Erasing trivial detached edges while still treating them as coefficient generators elsewhere can break coassociativity at just two positive vertices. Also, a fixed-arity inference constructor is not automatically an ordinary unrestricted Hochschild cocycle. The report uses typed constructor recurrences and the actual coaction laws instead of assuming that identity.

3. Exact inverse geometry and an effective residual

For an exact typed remainder R, every source T admitting R as a coaction remainder is obtained by visiting each leaf-edge occurrence of R and either retaining it or filling it with a positive, correctly typed material tree. Those providers may themselves be open. The coefficient is the ordered forest of the positive providers; retained empty edges contribute the coefficient unit.

This statement has both directions. A genuine cut supplies exactly these frontier trees, and every such assignment defines a genuine arity-preserving cut. There are no arbitrary external gaps: whole extracted roots leave typed edge components on the right. In particular, an empty remainder forest has only the empty source forest.

Suppose a finite observer recognizes admissible coefficient forests A, and a finite sorted observer recognizes a target property C. Compute the exact simultaneous states of all positive, pure, typed providers by a finite least fixed point. For each sort s let Es be those coefficient/target-state pairs. The inverse summary at a leaf is

U(es) = {(1, β(es))} ∪ Es.

At a retained rule, combine the child coefficient states in premise order and apply the rule’s target transition. At a forest, concatenate component summaries. Accept R precisely when no reachable pair has an A-accepted coefficient and a C-rejected source.

Finite residual theorem. Over a finite exact sorted rule registry, the universal residual of a recognizable material assertion and a recognizable source assertion under this typed CK coaction is effectively recognizable. Rejection supplies actual providers, exact typed slots, and a violating assembled proof. Provider size is unbounded; the finite state calculation represents every reachable observation class.

Correlated provider restrictions are retained by an ordered-word automaton or its transition monoid. Taking independent per-hole projections is not generally sound. Universal validity is also distinct from feasibility: an empty compatible provider class makes a universal condition vacuously true.

To consider only complete assembled proofs, use the target predicate “incomplete, or complete and safe.” Completeness means no proof meta-holes; object-language assumptions are permitted in their declared judgments. This guards the full coaction residual without claiming that complete trees alone form a subcoalgebra.

Staging is an actual coaction consequence

Let A⋆D combine coefficient assertions using positive support of the CK coproduct, and write WPA for the universal residual. Then

WPA⋆D(C) = WPD(WPA(C)).

The order reverses because these are right adjoints. This supplies coherent staged specifications over CK cut flags. It is not permission to discard correlations or to impose new restrictions on intermediate domains without proof. The finite observer states themselves are not asserted to form a Hopf quotient.

4. A logical application: surviving material after exponential normalization

Use a small positive exponential term fragment with a seed a:p, a counted opaque unary material operation g:p→p, an uncounted opaque binary operation m:p,p→p, promotion, and let !x=P in Q. The operations are free constructors, not computational effects. Unrestricted variables can be elaborated by dereliction, weakening and contraction at one ordinary exponential label.

An exact finite registry uses three interfaces: closed p, closed !p, and p under one unrestricted x:p. Consumers use x,a,g,m. Closed providers can contain arbitrarily nested lets. Therefore proof size, duplication demand and normal-form size are unbounded even though the interface registry is finite.

Independently of the CK construction, an induction on normalization proves an affine demand summary (c,d): c counts retained g occurrences intrinsic to the term, and d gives the multiplicity with which an external value contributes. At the provider/consumer boundary,

countg(NF(let !x=P in Q)) = cQ + dQcP.

For multiple free variables the summary is a vector, with the corresponding substitution formula. Clipping nonnegative values at budget B+1 is exact for the threshold test: capped addition and multiplication form a finite semiring. Crucially, zero times overflow is zero, because an unused provider contributes nothing to the final normal form.

The two-child let frontier therefore has the exact joint contract

cQ + dQcP ≤ B.

A costly provider can be safe with an erasing consumer; a cheap provider can be safe with a duplicating consumer. Their independent allowed sets would also permit a costly provider with a duplicating consumer, which may violate the budget. A consumer’s own scalar cost alone is insufficient: m(x,x) and m(a,a) both have zero intrinsic g cost but demand two and zero copies respectively.

Actual ancestry matters

let !x = !g(a) in m(x,x) → m(g(a),g(a))
g(let !x = !a in m(x,x)) → g(m(a,a)).

These terms have the same coarse constructor histogram. Their normal forms contain two and one g occurrences. This is an ancestry-sensitive logical observation, not a rule-support score. Complete typed rule decorations are not claimed identical.

Seven nested closed duplicators produce 128 g occurrences from one source occurrence. The finite observer detects overflow without constructing that full normal form. The direct normalizer was used independently to validate the example.

Scope. The guarantee bounds occurrences in the full normal form. It does not bound reduction time, peak space, or the effects performed while evaluating an argument later discarded. It excludes recursion, higher-order application, inspection of values, and material rewrite rules. It does not establish general SELL Cut elimination.

5. From one contract to every CK frontier

At each positive rule tree the analyzer chooses either its whole rooted subtree or compatible choices below its children. Selecting an ancestor excludes all descendant selections. This is exactly the CK antichain geometry; treating all vertices as independent choices would produce false frontiers.

The resulting finite set of states decides whether some or every selected frontier has a remainder satisfying the universal replacement contract. It can retain an offending antichain and actual violating replacements. Original-material selection and future-provider policy are conceptually separate; using one language for both is a tested special case, not a theorem requirement.

Nested installation uses the project’s existing masking theorem: an outer ancestor selection swallows the complete payload beneath it. The new observer evaluates that existing decomposition law. We have not renamed the previously proved simultaneous-opening theorem as a new result.

This is how CK does work here: it specifies the permitted decompositions and their staged composition, while the normalization observer supplies an independently justified consequence of the decomposition. The approach does not require normalization itself to preserve the coproduct; contraction already gives counterexamples to that stronger claim.

6. D1 remains a concrete two-calculus comparison

At a fixed subexponential promotion preorder, the prior completion replaces newly enabled weakening/contraction rules by old-rule macros with Cut. The source and target have separate proof carriers. Choose shared material constructors excluding the added modal, structural, identity and Cut scaffolding.

The macros preserve their input proofs exactly once, introduce no selected vertices and retain every typed proof hole. Hence their selected material cuts correspond bijectively, including nonprimitive branching factors:

ρTF = (id ⊗ F)ρS.

The inverse theorem now strengthens the comparison. Every material assembly above F(R) fills the same ordered typed leaves with shared providers, which F fixes. It therefore lifts to an assembly above R. For a target predicate C,

F⁻¹(WPAT(C)) = WPAS(F⁻¹(C)).

This is exact for the chosen shared material universe. It does not infer coverage of arbitrary new target providers from colinearity alone. Nor does it say that independently defined source and target properties coincide: the right-hand predicate is explicitly a pullback. We have not proved that these SELL repair macros preserve the separate normalization-budget observer.

The executable comparison checks actual repaired proofs, their typed residuals and literal refill, selecting both g and tensor introduction. Thus the factors include branching tensor(g,g), not just primitive labels. Material records agree, while full cut counts differ in every example because the repair adds logical structure.

Several tempting “discoveries” were rejected. Ordinary context preimages are standard automata mathematics. Semiring demand substitution is standard quantitative typing. The project already had duplication polynomials, correlated-provider examples and copy-sensitive cut analyses. The incremental result is the effective universal regular-provider residual and frontier compiler on a justified typed CK carrier, with the concrete normalization observation and exact repair comparison. Publication novelty is unverified.

Close baselines include Gaißert–Kufleitner on forest substitution, Atkey’s quantitative type theory, Accattoli on exponentials and Cut-elimination cost, and Vollmer et al. on mixed linear and graded logic. Their results are often much broader in their own domains; no improvement over their normalization or complexity theorems is claimed.
