# D4 and D1/D5: adversarial novelty and value audit

11 October 2026. Independent review. This is a mathematical scope audit, not a proof of global publication priority.

## Provisional verdict

The finite typed residual direction is worth pursuing, but **regularity of fixed-context filling contracts is established automata mathematics**. The worthwhile target is a concrete comparison theorem in which CK admissible cuts, typed boundary preservation and staged decomposition constrain a logical translation or proof transformation. Treat finite observers as the decision engine for that theorem, not as the source of its novelty.

The untyped round-2 inverse-cut recursion remains a useful explicit construction. Its E-bisaturated horizontal monoid is a genuine detail and its gap decomposition uses CK geometry. Transferring it to ranked typed rules is not merely adding colors: erased child slots, typed vacancies and endpoints must change the relation. A result about one fixed retained context can be correct while proving much less than the original universal inverse-CK-assembly result.

## Primary-source boundary

Gaißert–Kufleitner, *The Inclusion Problem for Forest Languages under Substitutions* (2021), https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02571v1, directly supplies effective inverse relational leaf substitution (Theorem 7), Boolean closure, and saturation of substitutions by finite observation classes. Consequently universal independent-provider safety for regular forest predicates follows by complementing the inverse image of the bad language. Their substitutions replace leaves independently; global correlations require a separately represented joint relation. This is a much closer comparator than generic decidability of tree automata. Their full complexity classifications should not be copied to a differently represented CK contract problem.

Kock, *Categorification of Hopf algebras of rooted trees*, https://mat.uab.cat/~kock/cat/bialg.html and arXiv:1109.5785, explicitly links P-tree decomposition to polynomial monoidal structure. Its unreduced bialgebra becomes Hopf only after the stated degree-zero collapse. Kock, *Polynomial functors and combinatorial Dyson–Schwinger equations*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03027, provides the typed polynomial-tree and CK bridge. Neither permits us to identify a typed identity edge with an empty forest silently. The parent project already records this issue, so rediscovering the warning is not a new result.

A first search-engine verification returned largely irrelevant results for narrow site queries; conclusions here rest on opened primary papers and author pages, not those results.

## Exact standard reduction for a fixed typed remainder

Let a finite many-sorted rule signature have operations r:(s1,...,sn)->s. Let Q_s be finite observation sets, and each rule interpret as a total transition on correctly sorted tuples. For a context C with distinct holes of sorts t1,...,tk, substitution gives a function

    eval_C : Q_t1 × ... × Q_tk -> Q_s.

For accepted root states F_s, the exact joint provider-state contract is eval_C^{-1}(F_s). For a joint realizable provider relation J, universal safety is J contained in this preimage. An offending realizable tuple gives a counterexample by choosing its representative providers. Context composition computes function composition. These conclusions use ordinary many-sorted term algebra semantics. They are useful, but adding the phrase CK remainder does not establish a new theorem.

What must be added to connect this to D4 is a theorem that the contexts and provider tuples are precisely the typed factors of genuine CK cuts (including side conditions), and that nested decomposition and filling agree with the advertised staged interpretation. Even then the observation may fail to preserve decomposition: equality of eval states is not a coalgebra congruence.

Finite signature is a substantive restriction. An unbounded logical formula language, unbounded contexts and fresh-name conditions cannot be made finite merely by saying rules have finitely many schematic names. Either bound the actual rule/interface registry, supply a sound finite abstraction with stated loss of completeness, or prove a symbolic extension.

## Recommended stronger theorem package

Combine D4 with the natural SELL completion compiler from D1. The compiler replaces a newly permitted structural rule by an old-rule macro with Cut, using its original premise once. Study **selected material extraction contracts across that translation**, rather than generic safety of arbitrary syntax trees.

The critical statement should have three levels:

1. Sound transport: every selected source cut maps to a target cut, with its provider tuple and typed remainder transported correctly.
2. Exactness: every relevant target cut of a translated proof lifts to such a source cut. For a material-only selection this can plausibly follow from the exactly-once macro and absence of newly selected nodes. It is false for unrestricted target logical cuts, which can enter the new macro.
3. Contract transfer: because the exact relation and observer commute, universal and existential decomposition assertions can be transported between the two calculi; finite recognizers decide the represented contracts.

The generic transfer lemma is elementary relational mathematics. The real mathematical work is proving the lift for actual proof-rule macros and showing the chosen contract expresses an independently relevant logical property. Full target-language universal assembly adds another obstruction: target contexts outside the compiler image need not lift. Restricting to image assemblies is legitimate but weaker and must be explicit.

This would use two native proof carriers, respect the user's comparison requirement, and make CK decomposition relevant to the repair translation. It would not show that subsequent normalization is colinear: contraction normalization can duplicate providers even though the repair macro itself is linear in its input proof.

## Claims to avoid

Do not infer Cut admissibility from a finite-state recognizer accepting rule trees. Do not infer semantic normalization or runtime deadlock freedom from a syntactic cut contract. Do not infer a full CK Hopf quotient from contextual observation equality. Do not claim polynomial time when a k-hole relation is tabulated with size exponential in k, or when determinization is hidden in preparation. Do not claim CK algorithmic indispensability merely because the same split relation can be written coalgebraically.

The legitimate mathematical aim is still substantial: identify which proof transformations preserve independently justified decomposition specifications, and obtain a complete finite procedure within an explicitly finite registry. That is narrower than reconstructing an analytic calculus solely from CK, but can be a useful bounded D4 result.

## Second pass: actual typed theorem and demand benchmark

Reviewed `typed_d4.md` and `carrier_audit.md` after their first complete drafts. The typed theorem correctly changes from ordinary inverse CK assembly to exact recorded-slot filling. Its finite-state recursion preserves the joint provider-word observation and the target observation together. Its all-frontiers wrapper uses the actual root-or-descendants alternative for antichains, and its staging statement explicitly cites the existing masking theorem. These are the right scopes; they do not repeat the erroneous assumption that any finite observer is a coalgebra quotient.

The carrier audit supplies a standard quotient-left comodule route from residue-sensitive P-trees to a connected coefficient Hopf algebra while retaining typed identities on the right. This resolves a real application obligation. Its half-erasure counterexample explains concretely why deleting typed edge residues only on one part of the construction is invalid. The mechanism is a standard construction, not a new Hopf algebra announced without provenance.

The proposed normalized-demand example is materially stronger than height or authorization toys. In its positive exponential fragment, the same constructor multiset can have different surviving material counts after duplication:

    let !x = !(g(a)) in m(x,x)     -> m(g(a),g(a))
    g(let !x = !a in m(x,x))       -> g(m(a,a)).

Both source terms have one g, one m, one let, one promotion, one a, and two x occurrences. Their normal forms have respectively two and one g occurrences. This tests ancestry against an independently defined normalization observation.

At a two-hole let boundary the condition c(Q)+d_x(Q)c(P) <= B couples producer and consumer. Finite capping works because all coefficients and operations are nonnegative. Zero times overflow must be zero: ignoring that would incorrectly reject an erased expensive provider. For a fixed finite interface registry the resulting observer gives a genuine unbounded-derivation decision procedure for the stated budget property.

The arithmetic is established quantitative usage analysis. Atkey, *The Syntax and Semantics of Quantitative Type Theory* (2018), https://bentnib.org/quantitative-type-theory.pdf, explicitly uses semirings to combine repeated and nested variable usage. Wood–Atkey, *A Linear Algebra Approach to Linear Metatheory* (2021), https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02247, develops substitution through semiring linear algebra. These are close conceptual predecessors; the present first-order affine demand lemma is not a replacement for their general systems.

Recommended final emphasis: **a complete CK-frontier contract analysis for a concrete nonlinear normalization budget in a finite typed registry**. Credit the demand arithmetic and regular-language engine as established. The CK-facing content is exact antichain exposure, address-preserving remainder contracts and nested-installation masking. The theorem remains a scoped proof-analysis result, not derivation of full analytic sequent rules from a Hopf algebra, not runtime cost analysis, and not a new general Cut-elimination theorem.

The independent SELL compiler comparison should remain a separate supporting D1 result unless its chosen observer is proved compiler-invariant. `contract_transfer.md` records precisely the needed observer and domain hypotheses and counterexamples to omitting them.
