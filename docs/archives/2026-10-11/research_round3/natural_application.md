# A normalization-aware typed CK contract from a positive exponential fragment

11 October 2026. Paper proof and executable checks; no Lean theorem or new general SELL Cut-elimination theorem.

## 1. Why the obvious SELL example was insufficient

Promotion legality depends on its displayed modal context and the fixed signature order. Once those complete interfaces are checked, a proposed ancestry-sensitive promotion-legality observer largely repeats typing. Label-support permission completion remains useful, but is not the promised D4 ancestry-sensitive test.

Instead measure an independently meaningful observation: **the number of occurrences of one distinguished material operation in the full beta normal form of an installed proof**. Exponential substitution can duplicate or erase that operation. This is not its original syntactic count, nor normalization steps, runtime, space, or total material use during evaluation.

The logical setting is deliberately small but standard: positive intuitionistic exponential proof terms, with uninterpreted first-order material constructors. The current theorem is about this fragment, not all of SELL or a process calculus.

## 2. Syntax, typing, and genuine normalization

Types are generated by an atom p, tensor, and !. A typing environment Gamma contains unrestricted variables. Terms are:

- x, a:p;
- g(t):p when t:p;
- m(t,u):p when t:p and u:p;
- (t,u):A tensor B;
- !t:!A;
- let !x=P in Q:B when P:!A and Q:B under Gamma,x:A.

The seed a and the unary and binary material operations g and m are fixed uninterpreted signature operations. Count g only. One can equivalently track all material operations using a finite cost vector, but that is not implemented here. These are ordinary first-order material rules p/p and p,p/p, not specially guarded rules encoding an arbitrary safety policy.

Unrestricted variables abbreviate a modal context with explicit weakening, contraction and dereliction at one unrestricted subexponential label. Promotion and let elimination have their usual interpretation. Thus the duplication in m(x,x) is actual exponential reuse; the term's two variable occurrences can be elaborated into explicit contraction. We have not built that elaborator, so the checked artifact is a term calculus checker, not the project's full sequent proof registry.

Normalize recursively under all constructors. Evaluate the provider of a let to !V, then capture-avoiding substitute V for x in its body and normalize. Closed well-typed providers of !A have the required canonical outer constructor. The absence of recursion and higher-order functions permits a structural evaluator with environments of already normalized values; material g and m never rewrite. The resulting normal value is therefore a term in the free material algebra, with no let nodes.

For open terms, the theorem below quantifies over closed well-typed values supplied for their free variables. It does NOT identify the summary with literal variable counts in an open beta normal form: a neutral let provider can remain stuck until its variable is filled.

## 3. Exact affine summary theorem

For each Gamma, define S_Gamma(t)=(c_t,(d_t,y)_{y in Gamma}) in N^(1+|Gamma|), with missing coordinates zero.

- a has all coordinates zero.
- x has c=0, d_x=1, other coordinates zero.
- g adds one to c.
- m and tensor add the two vectors.
- bang leaves the vector unchanged.
- For let !x=P in Q, assuming x is fresh for Gamma,

      c = c_Q + d_Q,x c_P,
      d_y = d_Q,y + d_Q,x d_P,y    (y in Gamma).

**Theorem (exact surviving-material demand).** For every well-typed t and every closed well-typed value environment v for Gamma,

    number_g(Normalize(t[v])) = c_t + sum_y d_t,y number_g(v_y).

**Proof.** Induct on the typing derivation while allowing every closed value environment. Variable, material, tensor, and promotion cases follow directly from the independent recursive normalizer. At a let, the induction hypothesis for P gives a normal provider !V whose material count is c_P + sum d_P,y cost(v_y). Apply the induction hypothesis for Q in the extended environment v[x:=V]. Its affine equation expands to the displayed let equation. This also proves that summaries are sufficient regardless of the internal shape of an installed provider, within this restricted constructor signature. No higher-order application, projection, branching on values, or recursive material reduction is allowed.

The theorem measures normalization, not just source bookkeeping. For example,

    let !x = !g(a) in m(x,x)

contains g once before normalization and twice afterwards. Nest this closed-provider duplication seven times and the end type remains p, while the normal form contains 128 occurrences of g. A body ignoring x instead yields zero occurrences, however expensive the discarded provider's normal form.

## 4. Exact finite budget abstraction

Fix B>=0 and define alpha(n)=min(n,B+1). Let K_B={0,...,B,B+1}, with the last value meaning overflow. Define u+_B v=alpha(u+v), u*_B v=alpha(uv).

Then alpha preserves 0,1, addition and multiplication. For multiplication, a zero factor yields zero even if the other is overflow; if an overflow factor is multiplied by a positive factor, the true result is again at least B+1. In particular **0 times overflow is 0**, not overflow. Thus the capped structure is a semiring quotient of N.

Apply alpha to every coordinate of S. Every constructor equation remains exact in K_B. A closed term meets the final budget precisely when its capped c-coordinate is <=B. Computing the finite state does not construct its exponentially larger normal form.

For a genuinely finite sorted registry, it suffices to use three interface sorts:

    C = empty |- p
    B = empty |- !p
    O = x:p unrestricted |- p.

Allow a,g,m at C and O, x only at O, bang:C->B, and let:B x O->C. Open bodies have no nested let rule in this deliberately finite registry. Closed providers may nest lets arbitrarily, so there is no bound on proof depth, normal-form size, closed cost, or open-variable demand. The only local context has one variable. This avoids silently treating an unbounded number of free-variable coordinates as a finite automaton.

The independent typed-D4 implementation uses exactly this finite registry. The more general normalization checker also checks tensor and larger finite environments, but those additional tests do not assert one finite recognizer for every unbounded type/environment.

### Explicit structural elaboration of the three-sort registry

Read C as the sequent `|-p`, B as `|-!p`, and O as `!p|-p` at one
unrestricted exponential label. The consumer variable is derelicted identity.
Its constant a is the ground axiom followed by weakening. Its g rule applies
the fixed unary material constructor to its premise. Its m rule combines two
premises by the fixed binary material constructor, obtaining `!p,!p|-p`, and
contracts the duplicated antecedent to `!p|-p`. Closed g/m instances have empty
contexts. Promotion sends C to B. The let constructor cuts B against O on
`!p`, yielding C. Thus its duplication and erasure are genuine contraction and
weakening, not a resource policy smuggled into a new guard rule.

Here g and m are free uninterpreted material constructors; their only equations
are the stated congruence/substitution laws, and they do not rewrite. This is a
specified positive term presentation with explicit structural elaboration, not
a theorem about Cut elimination with arbitrary nonlogical axioms. The direct
checker validates the term registry; the full elaborated sequent normalizer is
not mechanized. On a promoted provider the principal exponential Cut substitutes
its value into the consumer, duplicating or discarding it according to the
consumer's variable occurrences, exactly as the stated beta evaluator does.

The finite grammar normalizes by induction: normalize the strictly smaller
closed provider; its open consumer contains only x,a,g,m and hence has no further
let redex except those already supplied as closed values. Arbitrary nesting of
closed lets is allowed. The broader term checker has its separate environment
induction. Neither argument asserts a complexity bound for all sequent reduction
strategies.

## 5. Typed CK frontiers yield joint normalization contracts

Take an admissible antichain cut of a typed proof tree. Retain every hole's complete local judgment, including the position of a bound variable in its local context. Replacing each extracted subtree by its affine summary and evaluating the remainder recovers the exact summary of the complete proof.

**Proof.** Structural induction on the checked open remainder: an inference combines child summaries by the equations above; a hole returns its supplied summary. Admissibility guarantees holes are disjoint subtrees. At a let, the provider and consumer's environments differ; the bound-variable coordinate in the consumer is specifically the coordinate multiplied by the provider. Forgetting that incidence would invalidate the equation.

In particular, cutting BOTH children of one let gives an extracted provider P:B and consumer Q:O. If their summaries are c_P and (c_Q,d_Q), the exact budget contract is

    c_Q + d_Q c_P <= B.

This is a joint bilinear contract. A cheap provider may be compatible with a duplicating consumer; a costly provider may still be compatible with an erasing consumer. A single independent allowed set for each hole need not describe these compatible pairs.

Scalar summaries alone fail. Q1=m(x,x) and Q2=m(a,a) both have own material cost zero, but their demand coordinates are respectively two and zero. Installed against !g(a), the resulting closed normal forms have costs two and zero. Merely retaining the consumers' scalar costs loses the decisive information.

**Staged-frontier coherence.** Decompose extracted subtrees further by compatible CK cuts, compute their summaries in stages, then apply the outer contract. The answer equals computing the composite frontier contract directly. The proof uses the unique outer/compatible-inner cut decomposition already established in the project and associativity of substitution of constructor polynomials. Ancestor selections swallow their entire payload; inner holes that were swallowed are evaluated within that payload, not added as independent outer choices.

This is an application of CK cut flags to a normalization-aware observation. It does **not** say normalization is a coalgebra map. The one-to-two material duplication example explicitly warns against that conclusion. The CK coproduct supplies the family and composition of admissible frontiers; the finite demand algebra evaluates those frontiers before normalization.

## 6. Same local rule inventory, different answer

Consider the two checked terms of type p tensor p:

    T1 = let !x = !g(a) in (x, let !y = !a in y)
    T2 = let !x = !g(a) in (let !y = !a in (y,y)).

They have the same constructor histogram, including one g, two a, two lets, two bangs, one pair, and two variable occurrences. Yet their normal-form g costs are one and zero. Their ancestry and variable-incidence information explains the difference: in T2 the g provider is discarded. This is not a label-support invariant and not recoverable from rule multiplicities alone. We do not claim their complete typed decorations or origin assignments agree.

## 7. Connection with D1, D2, D5, D6

- D4: this provides an actual proof-normalization observation accepted by the finite typed residual theorem, replacing height and authorize/use toys.
- D2: nonlinear normalization is observed soundly and exactly in this restricted positive fragment, despite material duplication and erasure. General SELL normalization, recursion, higher-order application and step complexity remain outside the theorem.
- D5: for each bounded state contract, permitted provider families can preserve a quantitative normal-form property under every admitted installation. The contract can correlate simultaneous holes rather than impose independent policies.
- D1/D6: the previous subexponential completion macros remain useful comparative tests. We have NOT established that those macros preserve this observer. The existing exactly-once macro coaction theorem is insufficient to establish equality of normalized demand; a separate semantics-preservation proof or an explicit pullback of the target predicate is needed.

The most fruitful supporting direction here is therefore a sharply restricted D2, with D5 as its contract application. This does not refute the prior D1 analysis; it explains why forcing its support-only observer into D4 would have been weak.

## 8. Established baseline and novelty discipline

The following are established, not new claims: exponential substitution duplicates and erases; semiring demand accounting; graded substitution; finite bottom-up tree recognition; truncation for nonnegative budgets; polynomial context substitution. The project also already has resource polynomials, semantic brace/change constructions, and simultaneous-opening theorems. A fresh resource polynomial by itself would repeat that work.

The contribution candidate is their **specific bridge**: an independently normalized material observation feeds an exact finite compiler for typed CK antichain contracts, with contextual demand coordinates, correlated provider languages, failure witnesses, and staged-cut coherence, without assuming normalization preserves CK decomposition. Its strength depends on the generic typed compiler actually handling this instance; the independent implementation does so. No literature-exhaustive novelty claim is made.

Primary sources inspected:

1. Beniamino Accattoli, *Exponentials as Substitutions and the Cost of Cut Elimination in Linear Logic*, LMCS 19(4), 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15203 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15203 . This develops a substantially stronger explicit-substitution calculus for IMELL, including typed strong normalization and an actual polynomial cost model. Our final-normal-form count is much weaker and must not be sold as a cost-model result.
2. Lionel Vaux, *Taylor Expansion, beta-Reduction and Normalization*, CSL 2017; https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2017.39 . Resource-vector reduction and normalization commutation are established. We do not claim to reproduce or generalize that theorem.
3. Vollmer et al., *A Mixed Linear and Graded Logic: Proofs, Terms, and Models*, CSL 2025; https://kar.kent.ac.uk/112664/1/LIPIcs.CSL.2025.32.pdf . Semiring-graded substitution and explicit contraction/multi-substitution are a close methodological baseline.

## 9. Reproducible checks

Run `python research_round3/check_natural_demand.py`.

The test checker separately implements type inference, an environment evaluator producing actual full normal forms, and affine-demand computation. With fixed random seed 110326:

- 1,000 typed terms: exact agreement between normal-form g count and demand under closed environments;
- 6,000 capped-state comparisons, budgets 0 through 5;
- 3,000 random antichain decompositions, also re-decomposed inside each payload: direct and staged summaries agree;
- 48 direct joint provider/consumer frontier checks;
- same-histogram counterexample, scalar-summary counterexample, discarded overflow, and 128-copy fixed-type amplification.

These finite checks catch concrete implementation errors; the inductive proofs establish unbounded scope within the specified fragment. The staged checker tests semantic coherence on sampled genuine antichains; it is not a replacement for the project's separate coefficient-sensitive full CK coassociativity proof.
